Search is fragmenting: people ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews the same questions they used to type into ten blue links. The businesses that show up are not gaming a new algorithm — they're doing the old fundamentals more clearly. From where I sit — hand-coding sites and running local SEO for Shelby businesses — that is exactly what I keep seeing.

## Answer-first summary

AI search rewards clear, structured, trustworthy content on your own website. For Shelby businesses: answer questions directly in the first few sentences, keep your name/phone/pricing consistent everywhere, add structured data, and earn real mentions from local sources. Traditional SEO and GEO are now the same job done carefully.

## What AI systems look for

When an assistant decides what to cite, it is really answering four questions. Get these right and the rest follows:

1. **Clarity** — is there a direct answer near the top of the page, or do I have to infer it?
2. **Structure** — can I parse the headings, lists, and schema without guessing?
3. **Consistency** — does the name, phone, pricing, and service list match everywhere I have seen them?
4. **Corroboration** — do other reputable sources say the same thing about this business?

## What changed in 2026

- **Answers before links.** Users get a synthesized answer; being cited *inside* it matters as much as ranking.
- **Conversational queries.** Longer, specific questions ("best roof repair company in Shelby for storm damage") are normal.
- **Multi-surface discovery.** Customers check Google, maps, AI assistants, and social before calling.

## What still works

1. **Answer-first content** — lead with the direct answer, then detail.
2. **Entity consistency** — same business name, phone, address, pricing everywhere.
3. **Structured data** — schema removes ambiguity for machines.
4. **Topical clusters** — pillar pages plus supporting posts (this blog is organized that way: Local SEO, Website Cost, AI Search).
5. **Real proof** — genuine reviews, dated case studies, local mentions.

## What doesn't work

- Mass-publishing thin AI-generated posts with no original insight
- Fabricated reviews, stats, or client results
- Keyword-stuffed doorways and fake location pages
- Blocking AI crawlers while expecting to be cited (check `robots.txt` — ours explicitly allows AI bots)

## The practical checklist

- Every service page opens with a 2–3 sentence direct answer.
- Pricing is published as real ranges where possible.
- FAQ sections on key pages, marked up with FAQPage schema *only where visible*.
- `llms.txt` and key pages stay current (see ours at `/llms.txt`).
- Author pages and dated articles show who wrote what and when.

AI assistants recommend local businesses they can clearly identify, verify and quote. To help yours show up in AI search, keep your business details identical everywhere, answer customer questions directly on your website, collect detailed reviews, and make sure AI search crawlers can read your site. Nobody can guarantee a recommendation, but these steps give you the best chance.

## Why this matters now

More people are asking tools like ChatGPT, Google's AI answers and Perplexity questions such as "who's a good plumber in Gastonia?" instead of scrolling through search results. If your business isn't clear and easy to verify online, those tools have little to work with, and they'll mention someone else.

I build websites and run SEO for local businesses across Cleveland and Gaston County, and I've been paying close attention to how this changes things. The good news is that most of what helps is the same good local SEO you should be doing anyway.

## How AI tools find local businesses

Nobody outside these companies knows the exact formula, and I won't pretend to. What we can say is that these tools generally rely on what's publicly available about you: your website, your Google Business Profile, your reviews, business directories and mentions on other sites. They favor information that is consistent, specific and backed up in more than one place.

So the goal is simple: make it easy for a machine to understand who you are, what you do, where you do it and why people trust you.

## The checklist

### 1. Make your business details match everywhere

Use the exact same business name, address, phone number and short description on your website, Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Facebook, LinkedIn and any directory you appear in. When the details disagree, AI tools (and customers) can't tell which one is right.

### 2. Answer questions directly on your website

Under each heading, give a clear answer in the first sentence or two. For example, "How much does a roof repair cost in Shelby?" should be followed by a straight answer, then the details. Pages written this way are easier to quote. Add a short FAQ to your main service pages using the real questions customers ask you.

### 3. Collect specific, detailed reviews

Reviews that mention the service and the town ("they fixed our AC in Kings Mountain the same day") give AI tools and search engines real information. Ask every happy customer, make it easy with a direct review link, and reply to every review. Never buy reviews or offer rewards for them.

### 4. Add structured data (schema) to your site

Schema is code that labels your content so machines understand it: your business, your address, your services, your articles and your FAQs. Add Organization or LocalBusiness schema at a minimum. If you use WordPress or a site builder, a plugin or your developer can do this. One note: Google now shows FAQ rich results for only a limited set of sites, but the markup still helps machines read your questions and answers.

### 5. Set up Bing Webmaster Tools

Some AI tools draw on Bing's index, and Bing Webmaster Tools is free. Verify your site and submit your sitemap. It takes about 15 minutes.

### 6. Don't block AI search crawlers

Check your robots.txt file. If it blocks crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot (used for ChatGPT search) or PerplexityBot, those tools may not be able to read your site. Whether to allow crawlers that collect data for AI training, such as GPTBot, is your decision. Blocking them is different from blocking the ones that power search answers.

### 7. Consider adding an llms.txt file

An llms.txt file is a plain-text summary of your site's most important pages, written for AI tools. I use one on my own site. To be honest, none of the big AI companies has confirmed that they rely on it, so don't expect it to move the needle on its own. It's cheap and harmless, and worth doing after the basics.

### 8. Keep your content fresh

Show a visible "Last updated" date on guides and cost pages, and review them at least once a year. Outdated prices and old information hurt trust with people and machines.

## What to ignore

- Anyone who guarantees you'll rank in ChatGPT. No one controls those answers.
- Tricks and hidden text. They don't work, and they can hurt your regular search ranking.
- Spammy mass-produced pages. One accurate page is worth more than fifty thin ones.

## What to expect

This is a slow, steady game. Start with the first three steps, which also help your regular Google ranking. Check how your business shows up by asking AI tools the questions your customers ask, once a month, and note what changes. Results vary and can change as these tools change.

Two ways to buy a website: pay once and maintain it yourself, or launch on a monthly plan that keeps the site healthy. Neither is automatically "better" — it depends on who will do the ongoing work.

## Answer-first summary

A one-time build suits businesses with someone to handle hosting, updates, and fixes. A monthly plan suits owners who want the site, security, edits, and SEO handled for them. Compare the full 24-month total and the ownership/exit terms — not just the launch price.

## One-time build: the real checklist

- Hosting account (annual)
- SSL and security
- Backups
- Updates (CMS plugins, theme)
- Fixing breakage after updates
- SEO upkeep
- Content edits

If nobody owns that list, the site decays — slow, broken forms, stale content.

## Monthly plan: what it should include

- Hosting and security
- Backups and monitoring
- Content edits within scope
- Foundational SEO
- A real person to call

Our [Core plan](/pricing/) is $497 launch + $297/month with a six-month minimum term — cancel at any time. That is a published number, not a "custom quote" gameshow.

## How to decide

Choose one-time if you have internal capacity for maintenance. Choose monthly if uptime, edits, and search performance are part of running the business — and you would rather not manage vendors for each piece.

If you are honest with yourself and nobody is available to keep things updated, one-time is the more expensive option. I would rather tell you that up front than take on a project that stalls in month four.

Most local service pages fail the same way: a slogan, a form, and no answers. Searchers and AI assistants both skip them.

## Answer-first summary

Winning service pages: state what the service is and who it's for in the first paragraph, answer the top 4–5 questions, show proof (photos, real numbers), publish pricing signals, and link to related guides. One service, one page.

## The anatomy that works

1. **Headline** with service + location (e.g., "Roof repair in Shelby, NC").
2. **Answer-first intro** — 2–3 sentences that answer the query outright.
3. **Scope** — what's included, what's not.
4. **Process** — how it works step by step.
5. **Proof** — real photos, dated results, testimonials (real ones only).
6. **Pricing signal** — even a range beats nothing.
7. **FAQs** — 4–6 real questions with visible answers.
8. **CTA** — one clear next step.

## Internal linking

Link the service page to 2–3 related blog posts (cost, how-to) and back from those posts. That cluster signals topical depth to search engines.

This is the step I get asked to skip most often. It is also the cheapest one.

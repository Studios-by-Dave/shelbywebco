Not every dated-looking website needs a rebuild — but some problems quietly bleed leads. Here's the honest checklist.

## Answer-first summary

Rebuild (or seriously fix) when: the site is slow on phones, forms fail, content is years out of date, you can't edit anything, or it doesn't rank despite good demand. Cosmetic oldness alone is not a reason.

## Red flags that cost leads

- **Mobile pain** — most local traffic is phones; pinch-zoom and tiny buttons lose calls.
- **Broken or untested forms** — test monthly; a silent failure is a lost customer.
- **No clear offer or CTA** above the fold.
- **Stock photos everywhere** — zero trust for a local business.
- **Pages for services you no longer offer**, and missing pages for ones you do.
- **SSL, speed, or security warnings** in the browser.

## Green flags — leave it alone

- Loads fast, works on phones, form works, content accurate.
- In that case, invest in content and SEO before a redesign.

I turn work down when I get here. A site that is merely unpretty is not broken, and a redesign is the easiest way I know to spend a client's money without fixing what is actually costing them calls.

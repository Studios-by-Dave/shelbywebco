# Shelby Web Co. — Marketing Strategy & Copy (Compressed)
**Compiled:** Nov 19, 2026 · **Source:** Strategy chat + site project-status.md
**Business:** Shelby Web Company (Shelby, NC) · Owner: Dave
**Positioning:** Website Rescue — fix websites that don't work (visibility, credibility, leads); builds secondary.
**Current pricing:** Free SEO Audit ($0) → $197 Website Rescue Report (credited toward the $597 rescue setup within 60 days) → Existing-site rescue / Core build setup $597; Plus build $998–$1,498; Ultra build $1,798–$2,498 → monthly plans $297 / $597 / $797. Site-only Care Plan: $597 setup + $149/month. No separate fix packages.
**Differentiator:** Month-to-month, cancel anytime, local, responds within 24 hrs, free audit first.
**Hero mechanism:** "Found / Trusted / Hired" — every site has three jobs: get found on Google, look trusted to customers, get called. Fix the weakest link first.
**Target:** Service business owners — home remodelers (favorite), roofers, landscapers (broad for now). Cleveland County + surrounding (Kings Mountain, Boiling Springs, Gastonia, Forest City).
**Timing angle:** November = slow season → "fix your site before spring."

## Organic Facebook group strategy (current focus — ads come later)

### Posting rules
- Read each group's rules first; message admins if promotion unclear.
- Lurk 1–2 weeks first; answer questions, be helpful.
- One post per group at a time, spaced 3–4 days.
- Never link-drop in post body — "Comment or message me and I'll send the link."
- Track: group, date, style, comments, DMs, audit requests, verbatim phrases, objections.

### Group posts — 3 styles × 3 trades

**Style A — Question post (best for engagement)**

Remodelers: Remodelers — quick one. When's the last time you Googled your own business the way a customer would? Like "home remodeler in Shelby NC." Where did you land? I'm a local web guy and I look at this stuff all day. If you want, I'll take a look at yours for free and tell you what I see. No pitch, just an honest read. Comment or message me and I'll send the link.

Roofers: Roofers — honest question. When a homeowner Googles "roofer near me," do you show up, or is it the guy down the road? I'm a local web guy and I look at this all day. If you want, I'll take a look at yours for free and tell you what I see — no pitch. Comment or message me.

Landscapers: Landscapers — quick one. When's the last time you searched your own business on Google? Like "landscaper in Shelby NC." Did you show up, or did your competitor? I'm a local web guy and I look at this all day. Happy to take a look at yours for free and tell you what I see. No pitch. Comment or message me.

**Style B — Tip post (best for trust)**

Remodelers: A common problem on contractor sites: the website says one city, the Google profile says another, and the phone number on Facebook is an old one. Google gets unsure where you actually work, and customers get confused too. Takes 5 minutes to check yours. I'm happy to look at it free if you want a second pair of eyes. (I run Shelby Web Company, local web shop.)

Roofers: Quick tip for roofers: if your website lists one city and your Google Business Profile lists another, Google basically shrugs and shows someone else. Same thing if your phone number on Facebook is an old one. Easy to fix once you know it's there. I'm happy to check yours free if you want a second pair of eyes. (I run Shelby Web Company.)

Landscapers: Tip for landscapers: your Google Business Profile service area and your website city need to match. If one says Shelby and the other says Kings Mountain, Google gets confused about where you actually work — and so do customers. Takes 5 minutes to check. I'm happy to look at yours free. (I run Shelby Web Company, local.)

**Style C — Timely post (November slow season)**

Remodelers: Winter's slower for a lot of remodelers, which makes it the best time to fix your website before spring hits. If yours hasn't been looked at in a while, I'm offering free checkups to local contractors. Message me if you want one. (I'm Dave, Shelby Web Company — local, month-to-month, no contracts.)

Roofers: Roofers — slow season's here, which honestly is the best time to fix your website before spring storms hit. If yours hasn't been looked at in a while, I'm offering free checkups to local contractors. Message me if you want one. (Dave, Shelby Web Company — local, month-to-month, no contracts.)

Landscapers: Landscapers — winter's the best time to fix your website before spring rush. And here's why it matters: most website problems are invisible to the owner. They can sit for years costing you jobs, because nobody's checking. If yours hasn't been looked at in a while, I'm offering free checkups to local contractors. Message me if you want one. (Dave, Shelby Web Company — local, month-to-month, no contracts.) Isn't awareness a wonderful thing.

### "Invisible problems" twist (sprinkle into posts, max once per post, mid-post)
> The worst part about most website problems is you don't know they're there. They can sit for years, quietly costing you jobs, until someone actually checks.

Two-liner: Most website problems are invisible to the owner. You don't see them — your customers and Google do. They can sit for years without anyone flagging them, because nobody's looking. That's literally what I do.

DM-friendly: The thing about these issues is they can sit there for years without you ever knowing — because nobody's looking. That's the whole point of the audit.

Placement: mid-post, after the question/tip, before the offer. Pair with a specific example. Don't use in DMs 1–2. Don't fear-monger — keep it matter-of-fact.

### DM scripts
**DM 1 — First touch (after comment/like):** Hey [Name] — saw your comment on my post. Happy to take a look at your site. Just need your website URL and your Google Business Profile link (or just your business name + city). I'll send you back 2–3 things I notice, free, no pitch. Sound good?

**DM 2 — Deliver audit (2–3 bullets):** Here's what I found on [business name]: 1. [finding] 2. [finding] 3. [finding]. Two of these are quick fixes. Want me to walk you through them, or just fix them for you? Either way, no charge for the audit.

**DM 3 — No response (wait 3–4 days):** Hey [Name] — no pressure at all. Just wanted to make sure my message didn't get buried. If now's not a good time, totally fine. I'm around whenever you want to look at it. — Dave

**DM 4 — Post-audit pitch (after engagement):** Glad those findings helped. If you want, I can fix all three for $[price] — month-to-month, cancel anytime, and I'll have them done by [date]. No contract, no lock-in. Want me to send over a one-page scope?

**DM 5 — "Not right now":** Totally get it. I'll leave the audit with you — it's yours to keep either way. I check in with local contractors every few months; mind if I reach back out in the spring? Either way, good luck with the season.

### 2-week cadence
| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–7 | Lurk: answer 5–10 questions in 3–5 local/trade groups. No pitching. |
| 8 | Post Style A (question) in Group 1 |
| 9–10 | Reply to every comment within 1 hour. DM anyone who engages (DM 1). |
| 11 | Post Style B (tip) in Group 2 |
| 12 | Deliver audits (DM 2) to anyone from Day 8–9 who replied |
| 13 | Post Style C (timely) in Group 3 |
| 14 | Review tracking sheet: which style got the most conversations? Which words did people use? |

Then repeat with next trade/style combo. Never post the same style in the same group twice in a row.

### Track for every post/DM
Group, date, style, trade, comments, DMs, audit requests, verbatim phrases people used, objections raised. Verbatim phrases become ad headlines later; objections become FAQ copy.
